How much do solar panels cost in California in 2026? Real numbers, real payback.

Bruno de Madrazo
Updated July 26, 2026·How we calculate this

The 30% federal tax credit is gone. NEM 3.0 has been in force for nearly two years. Installer prices have settled at a new floor. Here is what an 8 kW residential solar install really costs in California today — and the payback math, without the marketing spin.

TL;DR: In California, expect to pay $2.65–$3.20 per watt installed before any incentives. For a typical 8 kW system that's $21,200–$25,600. With NEM 3.0 in force and no federal credit, simple payback now runs roughly 5.5–13.5 years depending on utility and self-consumption (it was ~4–9.5 years while the 30% credit lasted). It pencils very well in SDG&E (~5.6 years); it still pencils in PG&E territory (~6.6 years); it's marginal in SCE coastal areas with low usage (~13.3 years).

What NEM 3.0 changed (and why timing matters)

NEM 3.0 ("Net Billing Tariff") took effect in April 2023 for the three big IOUs — PG&E, SCE and SDG&E. It cut export compensation by ~75% on average. Practical impact for a typical home:

  • Export rate dropped from ~$0.30/kWh (NEM 2.0) to ~$0.05–$0.08/kWh average, time-of-use weighted.
  • Self-consumption matters massively. Every kWh you use on-site (heat pump, EV charging during the day) is worth your full retail rate ($0.32+ in PG&E E-1). Every kWh exported is worth ~$0.06.
  • Batteries went from "nice to have" to "almost required" if you want a sub-10-year payback. They shift midday production to evening peak.
  • Oversizing penalty: sizing the array 30% above household demand made sense under NEM 2.0; under NEM 3.0 you're selling that surplus for pennies. Right-size or undersize.

Payback math: 4 California scenarios

All four assume an 8 kW system at $2.85/W installed = $22,800 gross, no federal credit, and a year-1 production of 14,200 kWh — 8 kW × the 1,775 kWh/kW/yr California yield published in our Solar Payback Index dataset (NREL PVWatts: 6.08 peak sun hours × 365 × 0.80 system derate). That one number is the source of truth for every California figure on this site.

Two payback numbers per scenario, because they answer different questions. Simple payback = net cost ÷ year-1 savings, today's rate frozen — the conservative convention our Payback Index uses. Escalating payback and the 25-year net add 3.5%/yr rate inflation and 0.5%/yr panel degradation — the calculator's default cash-flow model.

PG&E E-TOU-C, no battery, 70% self-consumption

Blended value $0.242/kWh (70% at PG&E's $0.32 retail, 30% exported at $0.06). Year-1 savings: ~$3,436. Payback: 6.6 years simple, 6.1 escalating. 25-year net savings: ~$102,200.

PG&E + Powerwall 3 (≈+$11,000), 95% self-consumption

Blended value $0.307/kWh on a $33,800 net cost. Year-1 savings: ~$4,359. Payback: 7.8 years simple, 7.1 escalating. 25-year net: ~$124,800. (Battery wears out around year 12-15 — replacement cost not modelled.)

SDG&E EV-TOU-5, no battery, EV at home

SDG&E retail rates are the highest in the lower 48 — $0.44/kWh here, and a daytime EV charge lifts self-consumption to 60%, for a $0.288/kWh blend. Year-1 savings: ~$4,090. Payback: 5.6 years simple, 5.2 escalating. 25-year net: ~$126,000.

SCE coastal, low usage (450 kWh/mo)

Mild climate = low cooling load, so an 8 kW array is badly oversized for this home's 5,400 kWh/yr. Only ~2,970 kWh (55% of load) lands as self-consumption at the 35.25¢ California average; the other 11,230 kWh exports at $0.06. Year-1 savings: ~$1,721. Payback: 13.3 years simple, 11.3 escalating. 25-year net: ~$39,800. Borderline — most installers will recommend a smaller 5 kW system here, and they're right.

An 8 kW PG&E system, no battery, no federal credit

Inputs: 8 kW DC at $2.85/W installed, the NREL PVWatts California yield of 1,775 kWh/kW/yr from our dataset, PG&E retail $0.32/kWh, and the federal credit set to $0 (OBBBA, 2026). The catch under NEM 3.0 is that exports are not worth retail — so we split production into what you use on-site vs what you sell back.

System cost — 8 kW × 1,000 × $2.85/W$22,800
Year-1 production — 8 kW × 1,775 kWh/kW14,200 kWh
Self-consumed (70%) — 9,940 kWh × $0.32 retail$3,181
Exported (30%) — 4,260 kWh × $0.06 NEM 3.0$256
Blended value — $3,436 ÷ 14,200 kWh$0.242 / kWh
Year-1 savings — 14,200 kWh × $0.242 blended$3,436 / yr
Payback (no credit): $22,800 ÷ $3,436 = about 6.6 years on flat rates, or ~6.1 years once you add the 3.5%/yr rate inflation the calculator models. That is the honest NEM 3.0 number — not the ~5.0-year figure you'd get if you (wrongly) valued every exported kWh at full retail.

To reproduce it: open the Solar ROI calculator, pick California, keep the size at 8 kW and the production default at 1,775 kWh/kW/yr, then override the two fields this scenario changes — cost $2.85/W (a mid-tier PG&E-area quote) and rate 24.2 ¢/kWh, the NEM 3.0 blended value computed above. Then set the export-credit selector to full retail ×1.00: the 24.2 ¢ blend has already priced your exports at NEM 3.0 rates, so leaving the calculator's ×0.58 California default in place would discount them twice. Keep the federal credit unchecked. You should land on 6.6 years simple, ~6.1 with rate inflation.

Why this beats the 7.1 years our Payback Index publishes for California: the index applies a statewide 0.58 export value factor (roughly 50% self-consumption at the 35.25¢ state average) on a $2.58/W install. This scenario is a specific PG&E household that self-consumes 70%, which is worth 0.76 × retail. Same dataset, different — and stated — self-consumption assumption.

The honest verdict: does CA solar still pencil?

Solar still pencils if…

  • You're on PG&E or SDG&E, where retail rates ($0.32–$0.44/kWh) make every self-consumed kWh worth a fortune.
  • You can self-consume 60%+ of production — a daytime EV charge, a heat pump, or a battery shifting midday solar to the evening peak.
  • You pay cash or use a low-rate HELOC. At $2.85/W or below, the 6–8-year simple payback holds.
  • You right-size the array to your own load instead of oversizing to export at $0.06.

Wait (or go smaller) if…

  • You're SCE coastal with low usage (450 kWh/mo) — simple payback stretches past 13 years; a 5 kW system fits better.
  • You'd be exporting most of your production at the $0.06 NEM 3.0 rate with little daytime load.
  • A salesperson is quoting above $3.20/W, a 20-year 7.99% loan, or a lease/PPA — the financing eats most of the upside.
  • You're banking on a return of the 30% credit. Nothing has been proposed to restore it in 2026.

Which California counties have the best payback?

Three factors stack the deck for or against you: utility (SDG&E > PG&E > SCE for solar economics), insolation (higher in inland/desert counties), and household load profile (more daytime self-consumption = better under NEM 3.0).

  • Best: San Diego County (SDG&E + sun), Imperial County (desert insolation, comfortably above the 1,775 kWh/kW state average), Riverside/San Bernardino inland (SCE but high insolation).
  • Solid: Sacramento, Fresno, Bakersfield (PG&E, roughly at the state average, plus a heavy summer AC load that self-consumes it).
  • Marginal: SF Bay coastal, Monterey/Santa Cruz coastal (cool climate, low usage, fog).
  • Skip for solar alone: Coastal Mendocino, Humboldt — low insolation, mostly redwoods, fog. Better candidates for heat pump.

Cash vs loan vs lease vs PPA

The financing choice changes everything. Cash and loan keep the system as your asset; lease and PPA make it the installer's.

Method 25-yr net savings Catch
Cash ($22,800 up front)~$102,200Ties up $22,800 of capital
Solar loan, 20 yr at 7.99%~$79,300$191/mo; interest eats ~$22,900 of the upside
HELOC, 15 yr at 7.5%~$86,900$211/mo; ~$15,200 of interest, but secured against your home
Lease, 20 yr ($180/mo, 2.9% escalator)~$67,500$57,500 of payments; the escalator keeps climbing after the array ages; complicates home sale
PPA (15–19¢/kWh, 2.9% escalator)~$34,000–$53,300You buy your own roof's kWh back from the installer — the worst of the five

All five rows model the same system — the PG&E scenario above: 8 kW, 14,200 kWh/yr, $3,436 of year-1 value, 3.5%/yr rate inflation, 0.5%/yr degradation, so the 25-year stream of avoided electricity is ~$125,000 in every case. Only the way you pay for it changes. Cash and the two loans keep the array as your asset and subtract only financing cost; the lease and PPA subtract what you hand the installer over the term.

Tactics installers use to inflate quotes

  • "Free roof inspection" that conveniently identifies $4,000 in pre-solar roof repairs.
  • Battery bundled in the quote as if mandatory. It isn't (under NEM 3.0 it helps but isn't required).
  • Production guarantees with fine print that voids if a tree grows.
  • "Limited-time pricing" identical to last week's. Get 3 quotes minimum.
  • 25-year savings projection assuming 6% annual utility inflation. Historical California average is 4.3%; conservative is 3%.

What to do next

  1. Get your solar ROI calc dialed in for your real address and rate.
  2. Pull 12 months of kWh usage from your utility account (PG&E "Download My Data", SDG&E "Green Button").
  3. Get three quotes: one national, one regional, one local. Same system size, same panels.
  4. Compare $/W after all discounts, not gross sticker. Lowest $/W with reasonable equipment usually wins.
  5. Verify NABCEP certification of the lead installer.
  6. Read the contract's production guarantee and the escalator (if leasing).

Frequently asked questions

Did the 30% federal tax credit really go away?

Yes. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, eliminated the 30% Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) and the related Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25C) for systems placed in service after December 31, 2025. Systems placed in service on or before December 31, 2025 can still claim it; new 2026 installs cannot.

Are there still California-specific incentives?

SGIP (Self-Generation Incentive Program) still funds residential batteries for medical-baseline customers and in high-fire-risk zones — up to $1,000/kWh. The DAC-SASH program offers free solar to qualifying low-income households in disadvantaged communities. Property tax exclusion for solar is still in effect.

How much does a Tesla Powerwall 3 add?

$11,000–$14,000 installed in California (May 2026) — the top of the $10,500–$14,000 national band, because California labor and permitting run high. Bundled with an 8 kW solar install it's typically $11,000–$12,500 due to shared mounting/labor. Pencils best in PG&E and SDG&E territory where TOU spreads are wide.

Should I wait for prices to drop?

Module prices are at historical lows (~$0.12/W wholesale, May 2026). The remaining cost is BOS + labor + permitting + soft costs, all of which inflate with construction wages. Don't wait for hardware deflation — it's already happened. Wait only if a state-level program (SGIP, future federal restoration) is imminent.

Will Trump-era policy bring back the 30%?

No federal restoration of 25D has been seriously proposed in early 2026. State legislatures in NY, NJ, MA and CA are exploring state-level replacements; nothing has passed at the time of writing.
